Who comes to me

Most people who come to my practice are very hard on themselves, and when they first write to me they usually name one of three things: perfectionism and self-esteem, anxiety, or difficulties in their relationships. Once we look closer, these often turn out to be tangled together, feeding each other in a loop that's hard to see from the inside.

Whether it's a student caught in procrastination and disorganisation, someone going through a big change at work or in their studies, or someone worn down by chronic stress who wants healthier habits back, from sleep to routines, what many of them share is wanting concrete ways to understand and handle their emotions. Others come because they feel stuck, as if life had lost its sense of direction.

How online sessions work

Sessions are online, in English or Spanish, by video call on Google Meet, and they last 50 minutes. You'll need a device with a camera and microphone, a pair of earphones and a comfortable spot private enough to be honest and allow yourself to be vulnerable. Your comfort and your privacy come first, and you decide what to share and when.

Every session we build an agenda together. That means I ask first what you need or want from the session; sometimes you'll know, sometimes you won't. Don't worry, I'll always try to have something in mind. We try to keep it flexible because life can change one week to the next one, without losing our overall direction.

Once we know where to focus, whether it's something that's currently worrying you, or something you learnt or struggled with during the previous piece of homework, we do a "functional analysis", which simply means trying to understand more precisely what's going on and how to best address it. If we have time (depends on the session), we'll practice some skills or tools personalized to your situation (there are too many to name!) and you'll take a concrete exercise or complementary material into your week to practice or deepen your understanding of what was discussed in the session. Finally, we wrap up with main takeaways so you leave, whenever possible, with a greater sense of clarity.

Does therapy online work?

In my experience, for most of what people bring to my practice, the conversations, the exercises and the practice between sessions carry over to a video call very well, and the way we work doesn't really change.

Online also takes distance out of the picture: nobody needs to live near a therapist who works in English, and if you move cities or travel, we simply carry on where we left off.

It isn't the right place for an emergency, though. If you're in crisis or thinking about suicide, please call 112, or 024 for the suicide prevention line.

How I work

My main therapeutic approach is Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT), a contemporary form of CBT grounded in scientific psychology and behaviour therapy.

Many of us get caught in what we call the "control agenda": the idea that painful thoughts and feelings must go away before life can start. The trouble is that the fight usually makes them bigger, and our attention ends up taken over by the pain. In ACT we look at them more like a map, pointing to what matters most to you, and then we work on how to take steps, however small they are, in that direction.

Therapy in your own language

Sessions are in English or in Spanish, whichever feels more like home, so the difficult conversations never have to happen in a language that still takes effort.

I left my hometown about ten years ago and have lived in a few countries since, so living between languages and homes is part of my own story too. If you moved to Spain from abroad, I also have a page on therapy for expats in Barcelona.

Questions people ask

Does Spain have therapy for English speakers?

Yes. In the larger cities some psychologists see people in English face to face, and others, like me, work online, so where you live matters much less. I work online, in English, with people all over Spain.

What happens if the connection fails?

Very rarely, in my experience, and restarting the call or moving to a spot with a stronger signal usually solves it. If nothing helps, we can carry on with audio only, as an exception, or find another time for the session.

How much does a therapy session cost in Spain?

I can only speak for mine: €40 to €75 for a 50-minute online session, adapted to your situation through my intake form.

How can I check a therapist is registered?

Ask for their registration number and the college it belongs to, and look them up on that college's public register (mine is COP-CAT 36779, linked above). If you live in Barcelona, there's a page written for you: English-speaking psychologist in Barcelona.
